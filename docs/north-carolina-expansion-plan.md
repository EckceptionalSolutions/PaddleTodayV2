# North Carolina coverage expansion plan

Updated 2026-09-05.

## Baseline

North Carolina had 14 route records before this rotation. They covered 12 rivers, with three Yadkin River sections as the only repeated basin. The mix was 12 recreational and 2 whitewater routes. Thirteen routes used direct gauges and one used a proxy; only the proxy route was planning-only.

The route-candidate ledger contains 75 North Carolina leads. Two are already implemented and the remainder are held for specific evidence gaps, most commonly station-tied thresholds, lawful endpoint pairs, dam/release controls, current access, or complete geometry and camping packages. The holds are intentional and should not be converted into route cards by inference.

The current working set is 45 North Carolina route records. The new records remain planning-first where a nearby station is only context and no numeric recreational cutoff can be defended.

The Roanoke River State Trail is now extended with the designated Williamston-to-Astoria 12-mile section. This closes another named public access pair while keeping the upstream Roanoke Rapids station as proxy context only; the route does not infer a cutoff for the downstream reach.

This pass also adds three adjacent Mayo River State Park access sections and seven consecutive Yadkin River State Trail sections from Crater/Burch through Tanglewood. The official park and county pages provide named accesses, current access rules, GPS anchors, section distances, and mandatory-portage warnings where applicable; the route records preserve those as planning context and do not infer numeric cutoffs.

## Geographic expansion order

1. Finish the New River State Park chain only where named access areas, current facility status, and geometry remain verifiable. Keep bridge checkpoints and paddle-in campsites distinct from vehicle shuttle endpoints.
2. Continue the South Fork Catawba/Cambarus blueway wave only where the access manager, dam/portage, current endpoint coordinates, and route-specific hazard package are explicit. Do not invent a flow cutoff from a nearby station.
3. Extend Lumber River State Park and the lower Pee Dee/Lumber corridor one named access pair at a time. Require a selected endpoint pair, low-flow posture, wood/strainer controls, and current camping/access terms before publication.
4. Keep tidal/estuarine candidates separate from freshwater flow scoring. Require a named marine or estuarine corridor, lawful launch/landing, tide/wind/weather controls, and explicit proxy-gauge labeling before publication.
5. Treat the published French Broad Paddle Trail access chain as a distinct mountain-access wave; retain planning-only status unless the selected section has route-specific numeric guidance, and keep Pigeon, Nantahala, Cheoah, and upper Catawba technical/release-dependent candidates separate.
6. Use Deep River, lower Neuse, and remaining Yadkin State Trail accesses to fill the central and eastern coverage gap after the above waves clear their evidence gates.

## Publication gates for the next wave

- Named public launch and landing, with carry and parking status distinguished from a bridge or gauge coordinate.
- Direct gauge when available; otherwise a clearly labeled same-river or basin proxy and planning-only eligibility.
- No transferred numeric threshold unless a source ties it to the selected station and section.
- Explicit hazards for shallow water, rapid rise, wood, dams/locks, tides, private banks, water quality, cold water, and mandatory take-outs as applicable.
- Camping classification that distinguishes endpoint campground, on-route paddle-in camping, nearby basecamp, and no camping.
- Rights-clean route or same-river context imagery with a caption that does not imply endpoint depiction.
- Route-scoped canonical geometry, coordinate/water-distance review, overlap review, and a focused state test before the route is considered complete.

## Known holds

The current ledger holds most additional NC leads rather than publishing them. The largest hold groups are station-tied threshold/access packages on Cape Fear, Pee Dee/Lumber, Catawba/South Fork Catawba, French Broad/Pigeon/Tuckasegee, and coastal lowland corridors; technical whitewater or release-dependent leads remain separate until their dam, expert-hazard, and emergency packages are complete.
