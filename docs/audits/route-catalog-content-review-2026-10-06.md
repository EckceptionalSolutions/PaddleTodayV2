## Findings

- All 2,831 candidate public routes have a non-empty authored summary. There are **zero exact duplicate summaries** after whitespace and case normalization.
- Summary length is short on many routes: median **28 words**, 25th percentile **20**, 75th percentile **39**. Counts: 607 summaries under 20 words, 1,144 under 25, 450 between 40 and 59, and 240 at 60 or more. Short does not by itself mean interchangeable: examples name route distance, endpoints, hazards, access, or source-specific conditions.
- Only **8** routes have fewer than three source links; only **12** have fewer than three evidence notes. No route falls below both thresholds. Every public route in this catalog has a reviewed safety profile.
- **526** routes have fewer than two intermediate access-point records. This does not mean the route lacks its named put-in and take-out; those are separate fields and are present on the checked catalog entries.
- A conservative combined screen—summary under 25 words, fewer than three source links, fewer than three evidence notes, and fewer than two intermediate access records—flags **zero** routes.
- The former traffic winners checked against Search Console have specific source links, access notes, and route-specific summaries. The SEO worktree also adds reach-specific H1s and summary-based descriptions in the shared page template, corrects the duplicated “about About” FAQ wording, and adds a source-backed gauge guide with links from route pages.
- A freshness issue was found in the visible “Gauge context” copy: 22 route records (3 Wabash, 1 Goose Creek, 1 Clinton River, 6 Conodoguinet, 10 Yellow Breeches, and 1 Village Creek) stated July 2026 gauge readings as the latest conditions. Those values were nearly three months old on October 6. The route copy now keeps the source-backed thresholds and tells paddlers to check the current gauge and trend. The dated readings remain in expanded, explicitly dated evidence notes as historical source observations.
- The Willimantic endpoint review initially looked like a Route 66 access conflict, but the sources clarify that Route 66 is a valid earlier take-out rather than the water trail’s final public landing. The 2016 Last Green Valley guide documents Route 66 at river mile 21.4 and Air Line Trail at river mile 22.5; it identifies Air Line Trail as the last landing. Current Connecticut Trail Finder likewise names Air Line Trail as the last landing, and The Last Green Valley warns paddlers to exit there before the dangerous downtown dams. All seven route records ending at Route 66 now call it an earlier documented exit and distinguish the last public landing. Sources: [2016 guide](https://thelastgreenvalley.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/PaddleGuide2016xweb.pdf), [current trail listing](https://www.cttrailfinder.com/trails/trail/willimantic-river-water-trail), [trail manager access note](https://thelastgreenvalley.org/member-directory/willimantic-river-nrt-eagleville-lake-to-route-66-columbia/).
- A separate Willimantic distance discrepancy is directly measurable from guide mileposts: the Route 32 commuter lot is river mile 0.0 and Nye-Holman is 5.9; the previous catalog labeled that route 5.2 miles. Across the 34-route family, 25 distances differed from source milepost differences by at least 0.5 miles, with one as far as 3.5 miles. PR #72 merged as `5c36c3ba` and derives each route distance from the guide-milepost table instead of 34 hand-entered values. The October 6 frontend deployment now serves about 5.9 river miles for Commuter Lot–Nye-Holman and 6.5 for Commuter Lot–Heron Cove. ### Black Creek source and distance review

Seven Rivers Canoe Club currently documents the public Black Creek access chain: Churchwell-to-Big Creek (6 mi), Big Creek-to-Old Highway 49 (5 mi), Old Highway 49-to-Moody (7.2 mi), Moody-to-Janice (10.5 mi), Janice-to-Cypress (6 mi), and Cypress-to-Fairley (5.7 mi). It describes Ashe Nursery Road as an alternative landing about 1.3 mi below Old Highway 49, with a dirt drive, turnaround, and short path to a sandbar. Source: [Seven Rivers Black Creek guide](https://www.sevenriverscanoe.com/black-creek).

The catalog had material distance understatements where long routes skipped middle reaches: Big Creek-to-Cypress was 17.0 mi versus about 28.7 mi by the documented chain; Big Creek-to-Janice was 14.5 versus about 22.7; Old Highway 49-to-Janice was 10.5 versus about 17.7; Churchwell-to-Fairley was 33.5 versus about 40.4. The route source now derives all 28 page distances from an explicit chain of guide mileposts. Ashe Nursery distances and all combined reaches remain approximate because they sum guide segments. Time ranges were also raised for 20 affected long reaches; the 34.7 and 40.4 mile routes from Churchwell are now presented as two-day itineraries.

The source previously exposed internal “MDEQ water-entry anchor” labels for Churchwell and Ashe Nursery. The Seven Rivers guide identifies Churchwell as a public concrete boat ramp and Ashe Nursery as a sandbar landing with road access, so the route labels and landing notes now use those reader-facing descriptions. The Black Creek river hub now explains this access sequence and includes static links to the six documented adjacent trips. The hub already server-renders links to all sibling routes, so I found no broken internal-link path in this family. This is a specific accuracy and trust issue in the route content, not evidence that it caused the sitewide ranking loss.
## Route-pair expansion screen

I compared the public route groups with at least 10 routes by their put-in and take-out coordinates. Coordinates were rounded to four decimal places; endpoint pairs were treated as unordered, and coverage is the number of distinct endpoint pairs divided by the theoretical pair count for the family's unique access points. This is a screening signal for near-complete route-pair combinations, not a duplicate detector.

The pair count ignores direction, river geometry, portages, permits, hazards, and whether the trips answer distinct search intent. ## Curation decision

The source data does not support a blanket rule that short summaries or planning-only status should remove a route from the indexable catalog. Such a rule would exclude useful pages without identifying duplication or missing editorial support. Do not noindex a route solely because its summary is below an arbitrary word-count threshold.

The next curation pass should compare routes within each river family: exact or near-identical endpoint pairs, overlapping corridors that serve the same trip, and routes with no meaningful difference in access, hazards, gauge interpretation, or logistics. Keep distinct launches, take-outs, and route conditions. Retired routes should only redirect to a successor when the endpoint pair and user intent genuinely match; otherwise preserve the reviewed hold.
