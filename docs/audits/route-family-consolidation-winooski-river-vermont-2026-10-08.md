# Winooski River (Vermont) route-family review — October 8, 2026

## Finding and changes

Search Console Performance showed zero clicks and impressions for both the Winooski route prefix and `/rivers/by-river/winooski-river-vermont/` from April 28 through October 5, 2026. That is a small-family observation, not a diagnosis of the site-wide traffic decline.

The route catalog had 12 Winooski endpoint records. Three Floodplain Preserve combinations were already withheld because the stored point did not identify a defensible river landing. Review of river direction found two more combinations in the wrong order: Waterbury-to-Montpelier and Middlesex-to-Montpelier run upstream as listed. American Whitewater documents the downstream Middlesex-to-Waterbury direction. Those two records are now withheld until their direction, endpoints, and mileage are corrected from reliable sources.

Seven route options remain in the public trip picker. Keep three source-backed pages for the separate planning zones:

- **Marshfield–Gateway Park:** Vermont DEC describes the 24-mile Marshfield–Montpelier reach as boatable quickwater with Class II sections and several dams.
- **Middlesex–Waterbury:** American Whitewater documents this 6.4-mile, mostly Class I–II reach, including Hugo and Junk Yard rapids.
- **Bolton Dam–Jonesville:** The Winooski paddling guide documents this roughly 10.5-mile reach, the rapid below Bolton Dam, and a portage option. It describes an easier start at Bolton Canoe Access below that rapid.

Four overlapping but correctly ordered endpoint combinations now lead to their exact hub trip cards. Their trip records, distances, access caveats, gauge context, and hazards remain available in the picker. The two wrong-direction historical URLs and the three already-held Floodplain URLs lead to the hub's Winooski section guide, not to nonexistent trip-card anchors.

## Route access and organization

The Town of Richmond identifies Bolton Dam and Bolton Canoe Access as put-ins, and Jonesville and Floodplain Preserve as take-outs. It notes that Jonesville unloading uses the shoulder and parking is across the bridge. The route guide tells paddlers to check current parking and access before staging. The hub groups the retained guides by water and access conditions, and makes clear that the lower Bolton rapid changes the difficulty of the trip.

The official Friends of the Winooski access inventory describes Waterbury-to-Jonesville as a 10-mile mostly flatwater section, but notes limited take-outs near Bolton Dam and a steep portage trail. That source reinforces why a route cannot be inferred from nearby endpoint coordinates alone. Exact endpoint pairs without their own source-backed distinction remain trip-card choices rather than separate landing pages.

## Search Console and sitemap limits

The route prefix and hub both had zero impressions and clicks in the six-month Performance window. This evidence does not say whether Google discovered or crawled each URL. It also does not show a Winooski-specific sitemap, robots, or canonical defect. The site-wide sitemap index had a successful Search Console status in the October 7 report; do not treat that as proof that all route records are accurate.

The two reversed routes were a real route-direction and user-trust issue, separate from Google indexing. Their listing could make the route catalog misleading even if the pages were technically crawlable. Withholding those records and consolidating the valid overlapping pages improves route correctness and reduces duplicate landing pages, but does not prove that similarity caused the zero impressions or the broader traffic decline.
