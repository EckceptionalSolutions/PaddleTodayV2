## Source-backed planning notes

- The Iowa DNR map marks Cascade City Park, the Cascade dam, D61, Ozark Bridge, and Caven Bridge. It identifies the Cascade structure as a dangerous dam and describes the North Fork as usually paddleable during wet months.
- Jones County describes an approximately 18-mile remote stretch between access points, warns paddlers to portage around Cascade's low-head dam, and directs readers to neighboring counties for access. Its page says there is no access from a spot in Jones County. The City of Cascade separately lists a canoe/kayak landing and parking near River View Park, and the Iowa DNR map marks Cascade City Park; the hub advises confirming current signs and parking with the city.
- Dubuque County identifies D61 as a true access on Whitewater Creek. The route details explain that paddlers connect from the creek to the North Fork; the access is not represented as a North Fork bridge launch.
- Jackson County lists Ozark as an undeveloped access and Caven Bridge Access at 60th Avenue. Its water-trail map warns that unimproved accesses can lack signage, have inadequate parking, or be in poor condition. The DNR guide rounds Ozark–Caven to five miles; the route card retains its 4.4-mile planning estimate.
- USGS station 05418400 near Fulton is corridor context, not a route-specific safety threshold. Its monitoring page shows that reporting can lag, so check the latest observation timestamp before using it for trip planning.
- Five pages had a July 6 gauge observation labelled “Current gauge check.” Those evidence labels now identify the saved readings as historical snapshots and direct paddlers to the latest USGS observation timestamp.
