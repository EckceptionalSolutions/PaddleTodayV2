# Androscoggin River (Maine) route-family review — October 8, 2026

## Finding

The Maine route group had 11 individual route pages built from public-access endpoint pairs across the Mahoosucs and Greenway–Riverlands sections. Most used the same planning-only page structure, generic mileage and duration estimates, and USGS 01054500 at Rumford as either direct or proxy context. The Androscoggin River Watershed Council documents more than 50 access sites and describes several materially different paddling areas; an endpoint pair by itself does not make each generic page a useful standalone guide.

The 11 route URLs now point to their exact trip cards on `/rivers/by-river/androscoggin-river/`. No access-pair option was removed from the picker. The hub now explains the Mahoosucs, the Rumford falls-and-dams portage, the Mexico–Dixfield–Canton reach, and the distinct lower river and tidal planning needs. New Hampshire’s North Woods route group remains separate.

## Search Console

For April 28–October 5, 2026, the GSC Performance report showed zero clicks and zero impressions for the `/rivers/androscoggin-river-` route prefix and for `/rivers/by-river/androscoggin-river/`. This small-family result does not establish why Google has not surfaced these pages, or explain the site-wide traffic decline.

## Route and content boundaries

- The Watershed Council’s trail overview groups the Maine river into the Mahoosucs and Greenway–Riverlands, describes the downstream sequence, and identifies Rumford’s falls and dams as a significant portage boundary.
- Five endpoint pairs were oriented opposite the trail’s published downstream sequence. The cards now run Bethel–Hanover, Rumford–Mexico, Hanover–Rumford, Lewiston–Durham, and Auburn–Lewiston. Brunswick–Topsham remains an upstream option because the council’s tidal guidance says upstream travel is easier with the tide.
- Its self-guided paddle page names Gilead–Bethel, Dixfield–Canton, Turner/Gulf Island Pond, an Auburn out-and-back, and a tidal Brunswick paddle. These documented trips do not all match the catalog’s public-access pairs exactly.
- The Maine DACF inventory verifies named launch/access locations and publishes ownership and facility details. It does not establish that every site is open today, validate every route distance, or publish a suitable route for every endpoint combination.
- USGS 01054500 is direct telemetry near Rumford and proxy context for the farther reaches. It is not a universal Androscoggin paddling threshold.
- The current endpoint options and their card details remain intact. The orientation corrections follow the council’s published section sequence; they do not validate the catalog’s planning mileage. Before promoting a specific card as a surveyed or official itinerary, verify its distance, exact portage approach, and current access against the Watershed Council’s interactive map and access descriptions.
