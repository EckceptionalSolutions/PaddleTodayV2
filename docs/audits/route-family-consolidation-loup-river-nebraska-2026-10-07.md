# Loup River route-family consolidation — 2026-10-07

## Decision

The six records form the four-point Loup River Water Trail chain: George D. Syas WMA, Monroe, Pawnee Park in Columbus, and the ADM outlet near the Loup–Platte confluence. Keep three source-distinct public pages: the 8-mile Syas WMA–Monroe section, the 16-mile Monroe–Columbus section, and the 4.5-mile Columbus-to-ADM outlet. Consolidate the public Monroe-to-ADM composite to its exact hub trip choice. The other two Syas-start composites remain withheld from public route listings until fresh route geometry is verified; their old paths lead to a clear hub notice rather than to nonexistent trip cards. Preserve all six source records and access details.

## Condition and gauge scope

Nebraska Game and Parks reports a 350 cfs field test only for Syas WMA–Monroe. Keep that minimum-only cue and the live score on that upper section. The 16-mile Monroe–Columbus trip is documented as a six-to-eight-hour float, but the source does not publish a route-specific threshold; mark it planning-only. The Syas–Columbus composite also spans the unscored lower section. The ADM outlet is on the Platte River about one mile below the Loup mouth, so the three ADM options cross a gauge boundary. Mark those options planning-only and treat USGS 06793000 near Genoa as Loup context only. Only four of the six source records are currently public: the two longer Syas-start options have unresolved geometry holds documented in the route-coordinate review workflow.

The records also carried static USGS readings in their evidence notes. Remove those snapshots and use the live gauge link.

## Access, navigation, and private banks

The source-backed access chain includes an existing fishing access at George Syas WMA, the county-built Monroe access, the Pawnee Park Trail landing in Columbus, and an ADM recreation area established under a city lease. The Columbus endpoint uses hotel parking and a trail walk; confirm current owner permission and construction detours. The trail crosses shallow, shifting sand channels. Nebraska Game and Parks warns that river banks and the stream bed are private, so paddlers need permission to stop outside emergencies.
