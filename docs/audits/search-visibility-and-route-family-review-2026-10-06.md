# Search visibility and route-family review — October 6, 2026

This review combines current read-only Search Console evidence with the SEO worktree’s route catalog and overlap signals. It does not establish Google's unpublished quality assessment or prove the cause of the August traffic collapse. No production state, indexing requests, or external outreach changed.

## Finding

The sitemap, robots rules, response health, and internal-link graph do not show a current sitewide technical block. The August 19 traffic break aligns with Google's August 18–21, 2026 spam update; Search Console data shows impressions falling from 853 on August 18 to 124 on August 19 and 25 on August 20. Git history shows substantial route-data revisions and additions on August 10–14, followed by a large route-detail template rewrite on August 20–21; the first group may be relevant to a quality reassessment, while the template rewrite came after the loss began. A real Azure apex binding failure was found and repaired August 25, but its start time is unknown, and the Crawl Stats chart shows 0% server-connectivity failures on August 18–20. These are timing leads, not proof of cause or deployment timing. The existing investigation found no manual action or security issue.

For the ongoing indexing backlog, the strongest fixable lead is the scale and editorial distinctness of the route inventory: 2,831 route pages include 61 river families with at least 10 sibling pages (1,226 routes). Some large families reuse the exact same source-link set and have short summaries. Search Console reports that most sitemap-submitted pages were known but not crawled at its last indexing snapshot. Google defines scaled content abuse in terms of many pages that provide little or no user value, regardless of how they were made; that makes page-by-page usefulness the leading content-quality hypothesis to examine, but these catalog metrics alone do not establish a policy violation. See [Google's spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

This is a plausible contributor to today's weak crawl and index selection, not proof of why Google rankings collapsed on August 19. Relevant outside links are also scarce in Search Console's Links report. The previously strong landing pages remain a separate priority: earlier URL inspections found them indexed, crawlable, and self-canonical after the traffic loss.
