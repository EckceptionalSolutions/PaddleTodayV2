## Inventory status

Maryland was not previously registered as a supported PaddleToday state. The first systematic pass reviewed the Maryland DNR water-trail program, the Western Maryland corridor list, National Park Service route guidance, Frederick County’s Monocacy Scenic Water Trail description, American Whitewater reach evidence, and direct USGS stations.

## Reviewed holds and frontier

The following Maryland corridors were reviewed but remain outside the public route inventory until the same evidence package can be completed:

- Youghiogheny River: Maryland DNR describes the corridor as difficult, rugged, mostly primitive, and largely private. A defensible general-paddling endpoint pair and route-specific flow package were not established in this pass.
- Savage River tailwater: dam-release and whitewater consequences require a dedicated release-calendar, skill-level, access, and mandatory-portage package rather than a generic Maryland record.
- North Branch Potomac beyond the Oldtown–Paw Paw reach: additional sections need their own endpoint pair, boundary, access chain, and stage/discharge interpretation. The C&O Potomac inventory is now extended through Williamsport; further downstream sections remain separate candidates because dams, access type, and gauge interpretation change.
- Middle Potomac/Harpers Ferry: the inventory now includes the separate Shepherdstown-to-Dargan Bend Class I reach, using the direct Shepherdstown gauge, NPS/DNR Dargan public ramp, civic Shepherdstown landing, Antietam campground context, and the hard Dargan boundary before Dam 3 and the Needles. The Dargan-to-Potoma Wayside Needles reach remains held because its Class II+ hazards, cross-border access alternatives, and Weverton landing limitations require a separate expert-only access package.
- Baltimore Blueway: the current Blueway directory and NPS overview now add all eight named water trails—Inner Harbor Heritage, Canton, Domino Sugar, Middle Branch, Fort McHenry, Baltimore Peninsula, Masonville Cove, and Patapsco River—as separate bounded cards. The package uses city/DNR/Blueway launch anchors, clearly labeled same-estuary USGS 01589035 proxy context plus NOAA Baltimore tide guidance, and route-specific working-waterfront, shipping-channel, wake, bridge, industrial, 48-hour heavy-rain water-quality, campus-hour, and day-use controls. Connected landmarks, private docks, industrial shorelines, and unverified piers remain out of scope as landings.
- Remaining named frontier candidates: Upper Antietam Oak Ridge/Wagaman to Devil’s Backbone remains held because the upper AW access is informal/landowner-dependent and DNR describes access beyond Devil’s Backbone and Antietam Battlefield as limited. Lower Octoraro remains held because Horseshoe Road/Moore Road do not yet have a complete authoritative public endpoint chain. Middle and Lower Gunpowder variants, Beaver Creek, Big Elk Creek, Northwest Branch Anacostia, Broad Creek, Bennett Creek, Double Pipe Creek, and Little Gunpowder Falls remain held for the same missing public-access continuity, permission, endpoint, or route-specific flow/safety evidence. The ledger records each candidate separately so future source improvements can be promoted without losing the review history.
- Patuxent and tidal/Chesapeake routes: five Patuxent Water Trail cards now establish a bounded central/lower-river batch using official launch records, a clearly labeled upstream USGS proxy, and route-specific tide/wind, mud, water-quality, permit, camping, and daylight controls. Broader tidal/Chesapeake routes remain frontier candidates where access continuity or wind exposure is not yet qualified. The two Patapsco cards are bounded non-tidal planning exceptions: Woodstock-to-Daniels is high-water dependent, while Orange Grove-to-Avalon uses the 175-cfs float screen and explicit park-area anchors.
- Pocomoke River: five bounded cards now cover the public Snow Hill-to-Shad transition, the DNR-named Shad–Milburn 4.5-mile water trail, the Mattaponi Soft Launch alternate, and the separately mapped Corker’s Creek self-guided trail. The Pocomoke/Nassawango network still has unbounded extensions that remain excluded without a distinct public endpoint and route geometry.
- Janes Island State Park: the DNR guide publishes seven marked water trails, all beginning and ending at the marina, with lengths from 1.25 to 12.5 miles and explicit tide/wind/open-water distinctions. All seven are now represented as separate route cards, with the marina stored as distinct start and return anchors and the Crisfield Little Annemessex station clearly labeled proxy tidal evidence. No additional Janes route is implied from an unmarked marsh channel, beach, or private shoreline.
- Blackwater National Wildlife Refuge: USFWS publishes three marked paddling trails—Green, Purple, and Orange—with route lengths, federal Route 335 soft-launch access, the Shorters Wharf public-ramp connection, dawn-to-dusk limits, seasonal closures, and a geospatial paddling map. All three are now represented as separate route cards. The historical USGS 01490120 Little Blackwater stage-only station is explicitly proxy context with no current discharge or numeric safe-depth cutoff; no refuge camping or marsh shoreline bailout is claimed.
- Corsica River Water Trails: Maryland DNR publishes three mapped routes near Centreville—Mill Stream, Yellow Bank Stream, and Alder Branch—starting from the Town-operated Centreville Wharf soft launch. The official guide provides route distances, public Millstream Park access, sheltered-versus-open-water difficulty guidance, tide/bridge/mud-flat warnings, and a nearby Choptank USGS proxy is clearly labeled as watershed context rather than a local threshold. All three routes are now represented; private docks, marsh banks, and Grey Fox Farm are not treated as access.
- Kent Island Water Trails: Queen Anne’s County and Maryland DNR publish six mapped trails with named public access: Chesapeake Bay (Terrapin to Matapeake), Eastern Bay (Shipping Creek to Romancoke), Thompson Creek (Thompson to Warehouse), Kent Narrows North (Jackson Creek option), Kent Narrows South (Goodhands), and Cabin Creek. All six are now represented as separate cards with the official brochure’s distances, public landing permits, hand-launch/ramp distinctions, and exposed-water versus sheltered-cove controls. The North Trail Piney Creek alternative and Cabin Creek/CBEC extension remain explicit alternatives rather than silently duplicated route cards.
- Tilghman Island and Bay Hundred Water Trails: Maryland DNR and Talbot County’s official guide documents eight qualifying route options across Claiborne/Tilghman Creek, Claiborne/Wade’s Point, Bayshore/Lowes, Lowes/Cabin Cove, Phillips Wharf Front Creek, Phillips Wharf Back Creek, Black Walnut Cove, and Balls Creek. All eight are now represented with guide GPS anchors, route-specific wind/tide/shallow-water hazards, permits and landing-hour controls, and no-camping-at-public-landings posture. Harris Creek, Bar Neck as an alternate start, and other guide extensions remain explicit alternatives rather than duplicate cards without distinct distance and endpoint evidence. USGS 01491000 is retained as clearly labeled Choptank watershed-context proxy evidence, not a local safe-depth cutoff.
- Monie Bay, Fishing Bay, Eastern Neck, and Federalsburg: the official Maryland DNR/CBNERR, DNR/Dorchester County, FWS/DNR, and DNR/Town materials now add eight bounded route cards. Monie Bay’s three marked trails are represented as four distinct distance options; Fishing Bay’s two official wilderness trails are represented with the Island Creek route explicitly bounded; Eastern Neck uses the year-round Bogles Wharf loop; and Federalsburg uses the approximately one-mile town access pair. The Dames Quarter, Bestpitch, Island Creek, Bogles Wharf, Marina Park, and VFW access anchors remain subject to current field verification. The Choptank/Chester/Marshyhope USGS stations are clearly labeled watershed or upstream proxies, never local numeric safe-flow or safe-depth cutoffs. Unmarked marsh extensions, private shorelines, rest beaches, and longer Marshyhope continuations remain reviewed alternatives rather than silent route additions.
- Susquehanna and lower Eastern Shore corridors: broad waterbody names alone do not establish a safe moving-water route; public launch continuity, tidal posture, wind exposure, and rescue logistics remain to be qualified.

- Lower Eastern Shore and Southern Maryland water-trail families: the next evidence-backed batch now covers E.A. Vaughn’s three published distance options, Corker’s Creek, Nassawango’s two DNR-published options, Sinepuxent’s two named loops, and Point Lookout’s three named settings. George Island Landing is explicitly held behind Worcester County’s current rehabilitation closure; Sinepuxent and Point Lookout retain no-fabricated-mileage posture where current official text names the route but does not publish a stable distance. Other DNR-listed corridors remain frontier work until their endpoint coordinates, current access, route boundaries, and tide/flow/safety packages are assembled.
- Charles County Southern Maryland water trails: the official county guide and Maryland DNR Southern Maryland program now add four bounded route cards—Mattawoman Mattlingy Park to Smallwood, Lower Potomac Purse to Mallows Bay, Nanjemoy Friendship Farm, and Port Tobacco Chapel Point to Port Tobacco Marina. The package preserves the Mattlingy launch fee/no-wake control, Purse’s quarter-mile carry, Mallows paddle-in day-use boundary, Friendship’s primitive-camping posture and same-launch return, and Port Tobacco Marina’s private fee/permission requirement. These are tidal or tidal-influenced routes; the Mattawoman and Potomac USGS stations are explicitly route-context proxies rather than local safe-depth cutoffs.
- Calvert County water trails: the official 2018 county/DNR guide and current Maryland public-access layer add six bounded cards from the qualifying route family, with Beach Towns deliberately held because Chesapeake Beach currently lists Bayfront Park closed to the general public. The implemented cards cover Kings Landing/Cocktown, Hallowing Point/Caney/Benedict, Nan’s Cove, Jefferson Patterson/St. Leonard Creek, Solomons Island, and Marina West/Fishing Creek. Preserve the guide’s route-specific distances, carry/fee/parking controls, shallow creeks, bridge and marina traffic, open Patuxent/Chesapeake exposure, private shoreline, and current town/park closures.
- St. Mary’s County water trails: the official county/DNR package adds eight named routes—Wicomico Shores to Bushwood, McIntosh Run to Abell’s Wharf, Great Mills to St. Mary’s City, Tall Timbers/Herring Creek, Piney Point Creek, and the three St. Inigoes options for Smith Creek, Jutland Creek, and Kitts Point. The first three have route-specific one-way distances and intermediate public landings; Herring Creek remains a bounded no-fabricated-mileage setting; Piney Point is a 5.5-mile protected loop; and the St. Inigoes options use the guide’s 4.5-, 4.75-, and 6.75-mile figures.
