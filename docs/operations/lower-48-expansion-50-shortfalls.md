# 50-route expansion shortfall log

Prepared: 2026-09-15

This log records the current stop points for the 17-state expansion pass. A state remains below 50 when the reviewed public access sequence has no additional distinct forward pair that also has a scored flow profile, water-entry access, route-specific safety and camping guidance, evidence links, geometry and gallery support. Reversed pairs, near-identical access aliases and planning-only cards are not used to pad a state.

## Alabama — 20 slots open (30 scored routes)

The reviewed Hatchet Creek, Cahaba, Flint, Sipsey, Mulberry Fork, Locust Fork, Coosa and Tallapoosa chains cover the source-backed public pairs currently available. Additional delta, reservoir and bridge-right-of-way leads still lack a complete route-specific scored package or public on-water access.

## Arizona — 27 slots open (23 routes; 20 scored)

The verified Verde, Lower Salt and Gila Box corridors are covered. Remaining lower-Gila, Lake Havasu and lower-Colorado leads do not yet provide a distinct scored flow package with resolved carries, public water entry and complete safety evidence.

## California — 16 slots open (34 scored routes)

The American, Russian and South Fork American access chains cover the documented forward public endpoint pairs. The next candidates require a new public access point or a distinct source-backed corridor; reverse or near-identical beach combinations would be redundant.

## Connecticut — 3 slots open (47 routes; 45 scored)

Farmington, Housatonic, Quinebaug and Willimantic are covered through their reviewed access sequences. The two remaining Willimantic combinations would begin at the Eagleville dam boundary and add no meaningful paddle reach; they are retained as a research note rather than published as padding. The Housatonic planning cards remain below the scored publication bar.

## Florida — 1 slot open (49 routes; 46 scored)

The Suwannee expansion pass added nine distinct scored forward reaches and brought Florida to 49 public routes. The reviewed Stephen Foster, Woods Ferry, Spirit, Gibson, State Park, Dowling, Holton, Peacock, Lafayette Blue and Adams Tract chain has no additional distinct public forward pair that clears the scored bar without reversing direction or inventing an access point. See `docs/state-route-memories/florida.md` and `docs/operations/starter-evidence/florida-suwannee.json`.

## Massachusetts — 20 slots open (30 routes; 29 scored)

The Deerfield and Millers River files cover the documented public access-chain combinations, including dam-portage and alternate take-out choices. Further cards require new access evidence and a route-specific flow package.

## Mississippi — 22 slots open (28 scored routes)

The Black Creek landing chain is exhausted for the reviewed forward public endpoint combinations. Pascagoula and Leaf River leads still lack the complete scored access, safety and camping package.

## Montana — 8 slots open (42 scored routes)

The Clark Fork and Blackfoot chains cover the reviewed public endpoint combinations. Remaining candidates require a new distinct access or route-specific source package.

## Nevada — 20 slots open (30 scored routes)

The Carson, Colorado River Black Canyon, Truckee and East Walker chains cover the currently supported public endpoint combinations. Additional reservoir or lake options do not yet clear the moving-water scoring and access bar.

## New Hampshire — 20 slots open (30 routes; 22 scored)

The scored Androscoggin, Contoocook, Pemigewasset and Saco chains are covered. The Connecticut River access cards remain planning-only because no route-specific recreational flow cutoff has been established.

## New Mexico — 24 slots open (26 routes; 25 scored)

The Rio Grande, Rio Chama, San Juan and Animas files use the reviewed non-duplicative public pairs. Remaining Bosque, Ute Mountain, Taos Box and Razorblades leads have unresolved access, seasonal, low-water or expert-only constraints.

## Rhode Island — 3 slots open (47 scored routes)

The Blackstone and Woonasquatucket chains contain the forward endpoint pairs supported by their documented access sequences. Pawcatuck and Wood River currently have only the reviewed public pairings; the nearby Main Street take-out variant is already consolidated as an alternative rather than counted as a new corridor.

## South Carolina — 4 slots open (46 scored routes)

The Broad, Saluda, Edisto, Lynches, North Fork Edisto, Little Pee Dee and Catawba cards cover the reviewed public pairs. The Catawba addition uses the Fort Mill–Rock Hill–Landsford–SC 9 chain and official Landsford flow guidance. Additional routes need a new distinct access chain with scoring and safety evidence.

## Vermont — 7 slots open (43 scored routes)

The Winooski, Missisquoi and Lamoille chains cover their reviewed public endpoint matrices. Remaining candidates require new public access or route-specific flow evidence.

## Washington — 20 slots open (30 routes; 21 scored)

The scored Yakima, Snoqualmie, Skagit and Stillaguamish chains are covered. Spokane River access is useful and popular, but its remaining combinations are planning-only until a route-specific numeric recreational flow package is established.

Louisiana and Oregon currently meet or exceed 50 routes. This log is a research stop point, not a prohibition on future additions: resume a state when new authoritative access, scoring, safety, camping or imagery evidence clears the publication bar.
