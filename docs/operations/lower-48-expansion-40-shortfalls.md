# 40-route expansion shortfall log

Prepared: 2026-09-13

This log records states intentionally left below 40 routes after a score-first review. A remaining slot stays open when the next candidate would duplicate a documented endpoint pairing or lacks a route-specific scored flow package, public on-water access, complete safety and camping guidance, gallery support, or a reliable distinct reach.

## Alabama — 10 slots open (30 routes)

The catalog covers the documented Hatchet Creek, Cahaba, Flint, Sipsey, Mulberry Fork, Locust Fork, Coosa and Harold Banks Tallapoosa corridors. Existing files use the available public endpoint pairings for those source-backed sections, including Tallapoosa access alternatives grouped with consolidation metadata. Additional delta, reservoir or bridge-right-of-way leads do not currently provide a new combination with the same direct-gauge, public water-entry, safety and camping evidence. Do not pad the state with reversed pairs or unverified private-bank access.

## Arizona — 17 slots open (23 routes)

The verified Verde, Lower Salt and Gila Box corridors are covered. Remaining lower-Gila, Lake Havasu and lower-Colorado leads have public boating access but lack a route-specific scored flow package or carry unresolved fence, steep-bank, reservoir or limited-exit hazards. The existing research record is in `docs/operations/lower-48-expansion-30-shortfalls.md`; no unsupported lake loops or duplicate endpoint combinations should be added.

## California — 6 slots open (34 routes)

The scored American River and Russian River access chains now cover their documented forward public endpoint pairings. The remaining slots would require a new public access point or a distinct source-backed corridor; reverse or near-identical beach combinations would be redundant. Keep the existing seasonal-dam, water-quality, cold-water and public-access controls in force.

## Massachusetts — 10 slots open (30 routes)

The scored Deerfield and Millers River cards cover the documented public access-chain combinations, including dam-portage and alternate take-out choices. Further cards would either repeat nearby access alternatives or require new lower/upper access evidence and a route-specific flow package. Do not infer public access from utility, railroad or private-bank coordinates.

## Mississippi — 12 slots open (28 routes)

The Black Creek landing chain is exhausted in every forward public endpoint pairing. Reversed pairs and additional combinations would be redundant. Pascagoula and Leaf River leads remain research candidates, but the reviewed material does not yet provide the complete route-specific scoring, access, safety and camping package required for scored publication. See the existing shortfall record for source context.

## Nevada — 10 slots open (30 routes)

The scored Carson, Colorado River Black Canyon, Truckee and East Walker corridors use the documented public endpoint combinations currently supported by direct gauges and route-specific flow guidance. Additional reservoir or lake options do not yet clear the same moving-water scoring and access bar; do not create duplicate Truckee, Carson or Black Canyon pairings.

## New Hampshire — 10 slots open (30 routes; 22 scored)

The scored Androscoggin, Contoocook, Pemigewasset and Saco endpoint sets are covered. The remaining Connecticut River access chain is public and useful, but its current cards remain planning-only because no route-specific recreational flow cutoff has been established. Keep those planning cards discoverable and do not transfer a generic gauge band to score them.

## New Mexico — 14 slots open (26 routes; 25 scored)

The reviewed Rio Grande, Rio Chama, San Juan and Animas corridors use the documented non-duplicative public endpoint pairings that meet the publication bar. Remaining Bosque, Ute Mountain, Taos Box and Razorblades leads have limited access, seasonal or steep carries, low-water concerns, or expert-only whitewater without a complete scored starter package. See `docs/operations/lower-48-expansion-30-shortfalls.md` for the source-backed research record.

## Washington — 10 slots open (30 routes; 21 scored)

The scored Yakima Canyon, Snoqualmie, Skagit and Stillaguamish endpoint sets are covered. Spokane River adds public access and strong local interest, but the remaining combinations lack a route-specific numeric recreational cutoff and therefore remain planning-only. Additional lower-Columbia or Spokane cards require new threshold and access evidence before scoring; do not duplicate the existing public endpoint pairs.

The other eight target states now meet the 40-route target: Connecticut, Florida, Louisiana, Montana, Oregon, Rhode Island, South Carolina and Vermont. This log is a research stop point, not a prohibition on future additions: a state can resume expansion when new authoritative access, scoring, safety, camping or imagery evidence becomes available.
