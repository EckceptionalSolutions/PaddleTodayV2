# Lower 48 outside-17 route-capacity assessment

Prepared: 2026-09-20
Scope: the 31 lower-48 states outside the original 17-state expansion campaign.

This assessment uses the current route catalog through `scripts/report-route-universe.ts`, with `publicRoutes` as the 50-route measure and `scoredRoutes` shown separately. A public route is either scored or an explicitly reviewed planning route. Coordinate-withheld inventory is not counted as public coverage. The saturation and state audit records are used to distinguish an existing count from a credible expansion opportunity.

## Result

Thirteen states outside the original 17 already meet or exceed 50 public routes: Georgia, Idaho, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Minnesota, Missouri, New York, Pennsylvania, Texas, Wisconsin, and West Virginia.

Seven states already meet or exceed 50 scored routes: Georgia, Iowa, Kentucky, Minnesota, Missouri, Texas, and Wisconsin. Idaho is close at 47 scored routes, but its remaining inventory is mostly planning or whitewater material and has not cleared the scoring and safety gates.

Among states below 50 public routes, North Carolina is the strongest next expansion, followed by Virginia and Colorado. Arkansas is only five public routes short, but its gap is primarily a coordinate/publication problem after a completed saturation pass. New Jersey, Michigan, and Wyoming have plausible longer-term capacity, while the remaining states do not currently have enough cleared corridor evidence to justify a 50-route target.

## Recommended priority

1. **North Carolina**: add five or fewer distinct routes from the deferred French Broad, Lumber, New River, Deep, Smith, and lower-Neuse families after current access and threshold rechecks.
2. **Virginia**: use the DWR access inventory to work toward 50, keeping scored routes ahead of planning cards and avoiding lake/open-water records.
3. **Colorado**: run the required discovery sweep, starting with Arkansas Headwaters and other named public access chains that can clear station-specific thresholds.
4. **Arkansas**: treat 50 as a remediation milestone; review the ten coordinate-withheld inventory records before adding new river families.
5. **New Jersey / Michigan / Wyoming**: keep as conditional research queues, not active 50-route goals, until endpoint and direct-gauge evidence improves.

The remaining below-50 states should stay on a bounded discovery list. Their current records show too few qualified public corridors, too many durable threshold/access blockers, or a product mismatch with moving-water route scoring. Do not use lake, tidal, reservoir, or repeated endpoint permutations to fill the gap.
